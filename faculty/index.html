Faculty & Guest Speakers · Season One

Practitioners.
Not presenters.

Every River Hub session is led by someone who has done the work — not someone who teaches about it. Our faculty and guest speakers are drawn from the institutions and firms that define the field. Names are confirmed and shared with registered participants ahead of each session.

Every faculty member below is confirmed. Names are disclosed to registered participants 10 days before each session. River Hub withholds names by design — not because speakers are unconfirmed, but because calibre should speak before personality does. You see the institution, the track record, and the mandate. The name follows when you're in the room.

Why it matters

No career
academics.
Ever.

River Hub has a single faculty rule: every person who stands in the room must have done the work they are teaching. Not studied it, not published about it — done it. That means people who have signed DFI term sheets, argued with LP advisory committees, and made calls under real time pressure.

This is not snobbery about PhDs. It is a recognition that the tacit knowledge — what DFIs actually want to see, what makes LPs walk away, what the due diligence call really feels like — can only be transmitted by people who have it.

01
Active mandate, not emeritus
Every faculty member is currently working — not retired from the field. What they share reflects what is happening in deals and processes right now, not what was true five years ago.
02
Institutional authority
Faculty hold roles at institutions with real mandates — DFIs with capital to deploy, LPs with allocations to make, law firms with deals to close. Their perspective carries authority because they make decisions, not recommendations.
03
The room knows
River Hub participants are mid-to-senior professionals. They can tell immediately whether the person in front of them has done the work. Faculty who have not done it lose the room within minutes. River Hub faculty never do.
04
Name disclosed on registration
We hold faculty names until 10 days before the session by design — so the decision to attend is made on substance, not celebrity. Once you register, you know exactly who is in the room with you.
